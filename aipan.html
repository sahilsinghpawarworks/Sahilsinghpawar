Aipan

ResearchVisual BrandingUX DesignStrategy Design

(Scroll for more)↓

Craft is Aipan folk art, Kumaon, Uttarakhand

Focus is Brand identity & experience

Method is Discover · Define · Design

(Details)

To position Uttarakhand's GI-tagged folk art Aipan for global recognition. The project focuses on creating an intuitive digital experience and a strong brand identity that communicate authenticity, heritage and market relevance.

Methods: Survey, stakeholder mapping, empathy map, AEIOU, persona, customer journey, card sorting, value proposition

(A closer look)

(01)Context

Aipan is a traditional ritual folk art of the Kumaon region, especially Almora and Nainital, where it decorates entrances, walls and places of worship during festivals and ceremonies. White patterns of rice paste (biswar) are drawn by hand on a red earth (geru) base, on walls, floors and cloth. Centred on the bindu, the starting and ending point of all Aipan, it is practised as a meditative idea of noble living, not just decoration.

In 2021 Aipan received the Geographical Indication (GI) tag, recognising it as an exclusive cultural product of Kumaon, protecting it from imitation and aiming to raise artisans' incomes and its national and international profile.

(02)The problem

Knowledge of the art is thinning across generations, artisans have limited access to wider audiences, and there are few platforms that teach the right techniques and share accurate information. How might we carry Aipan beyond its traditional settings without losing its meaning?

(03)The process

Discover

We mapped Aipan's people, places, practices and products, and its motifs, from Bindu and Swastika to Shankh, Surya and Lakshmi. A survey of respondents aged 16 to 30 showed most discovered Aipan through social media (80%), at home or festivals (57%), through products (42%) and at school or college (22%). They linked it with calm and spirituality, festive nostalgia and a connection to heritage, and wanted workshops, products, cultural visits and short videos.

Define

Stakeholder mapping, an empathy map and an AEIOU study led to a persona: Meena Joshi, an 80-year-old Aipan artist from Almora whose family has practised the art for generations. Her goal is to preserve the tradition; her frustration is limited reach and accurate knowledge among the next generation. The question became: how might we transform Aipan from a traditional artform into a wellness experience that promotes mindful living, globally?

(04)The solution

We designed a brand essence, persona and value proposition that position Aipan as a mindful, ethical wellness experience, and a customer journey from awareness and consideration to purchase, retention and advocacy. Card-sorting the physical, digital and phygital touchpoints shaped the channels:

  • A social media campaign, government initiatives and global exhibitions.
  • Collaborations with wellness centres, including interactive physical spaces.
  • Hospitality collaborations with a welcome kit for guests: an Aipan painting, a pamphlet and discount coupons.
